Manatee County is building faster than almost anywhere in Florida. That is genuinely good news if you want a house that insures well and has a roof nobody will argue about. It also means you are negotiating against a company that does this professionally, every day, with its own paperwork.

What I Do On A New Build

The parts the model home does not cover.

Read the contract before you sign it

Builder contracts are not the standard Florida forms. Completion timing, deposit risk, warranty terms, and what happens if the appraisal comes in low are all defined differently. I go through it with you and flag what is negotiable, because some of it is.

Work out the real monthly number

Base price is not the number. Lot premium, structural options, design center selections, HOA dues, and the CDD assessment on the tax bill are the number. I build that out before you commit so there are no surprises at the design center.

Push back on the lender incentive

Builders often offer meaningful incentives to use their preferred lender. Sometimes that is genuinely the better deal and sometimes the rate makes up the difference elsewhere. We compare it against an outside quote rather than assuming either way.

Get an independent inspection anyway

A new house is not a perfect house. I recommend an independent inspection before drywall goes up and another before closing. Municipal inspections check code compliance, which is not the same as checking workmanship.

Questions

New construction, answered.

Do I need an agent if I am buying directly from a builder?

The person sitting in the model home works for the builder. They are pleasant, they are knowledgeable, and they represent the seller. Having your own representation generally costs you nothing because the builder typically pays it, but almost every builder requires that your agent be registered with you on your first visit to the community. If you tour alone first and bring me in later, many builders will refuse to recognize the representation. That single detail costs more buyers their representation than anything else.

Is a builder contract different from a normal purchase contract?

Substantially. Builders use their own contracts rather than the standard Florida forms, and those documents are written to protect the builder. Completion dates are often not firm commitments, deposits may be at greater risk, the warranty terms and dispute process are defined by the builder, and your ability to walk away after inspection is usually narrower than in a resale. None of that makes new construction a bad choice. It makes reading the contract before signing it essential.

What is a CDD and why does every new community here have one?

A Community Development District is a special taxing district that financed the roads, utilities and amenities of the community. Homeowners repay that debt through an assessment on the annual property tax bill, on top of HOA dues, often for decades. It is why so much new construction in Parrish and Lakewood Ranch can be priced attractively, since some of the infrastructure cost sits outside the sale price. It is a real number and it belongs in your monthly math from the beginning.

Should I buy new or buy resale?

New construction gives you current building codes, a new roof, impact rated windows in most cases, and generally easier and cheaper insurance, which on the Gulf Coast is worth real money. Resale gives you mature landscaping, larger lots in many areas, a shorter drive to the water, no construction traffic, and often no CDD. The honest answer is that new tends to win on carrying cost and resale tends to win on location, and which matters more depends entirely on how you plan to use the house.

General information about buying new construction in Manatee County, not legal advice. Builder contracts, warranty terms, and CDD obligations are binding legal documents and should be reviewed by an attorney before you sign. Builder incentive and financing terms change frequently and should be confirmed directly with the builder.

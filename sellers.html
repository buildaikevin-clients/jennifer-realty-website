Questions

What sellers ask most.

How do you decide what my home is worth?

Jennifer walks the property, then pulls what has actually closed near you in the last few months rather than what people are asking. Asking prices tell you what sellers hope for. Closed sales tell you what buyers paid. From there she adjusts for the things that move value on the Gulf Coast specifically, which are roof age, elevation and flood zone, whether the windows are impact rated, water access, and for condominiums the health of the association. Then she gives you a range and her honest read on where in that range your home lands.

What should I fix before listing?

In Florida the roof is usually the first question, because it is the thing most likely to stop a sale. Many carriers will not write a new policy on an older shingle roof, and if a buyer cannot get insured they cannot close. Age of the water heater and the electrical panel matter for the same reason. After that it is the ordinary things, which are paint, landscaping, and getting the clutter out so the rooms read as large as they are. She would rather tell you to skip a renovation than watch you spend money you will not get back.

What do I have to disclose when I sell in Florida?

Florida sellers have a duty to disclose known conditions that materially affect the value of the property and that a buyer could not readily see. Since October 2024 there is also a specific requirement to disclose flood history on residential sales, including past flood damage and any flood related insurance claims or federal assistance received. Her advice is always the same, which is to disclose more than you think you need to. Disclosure protects you. A buyer who learns about something after closing is a lawsuit, and a buyer who learns about it up front is usually just a buyer.

How long are homes taking to sell here?

It varies more by segment than by season now. Well priced mainland homes in the ranges most buyers shop tend to move in weeks. Island property, higher price points, and units in associations with assessment questions take considerably longer, sometimes several months. The single largest factor is still the list price. A home priced correctly gets attention in the first two weeks, and a home priced hopefully tends to sit and then sell for less than it would have originally.

What does it cost to sell?

Plan on brokerage compensation, title and closing costs, documentary stamp tax on the deed, any county or municipal items, prorated taxes, and whatever you agree to credit the buyer after inspection. Commission is negotiable and always has been, and since the 2024 industry settlement the way buyer agent compensation is handled is discussed and agreed up front rather than assumed. When she gives you a valuation she gives you an estimated net sheet with it, so you see the number you walk away with and not just the list price.

General information about selling in Manatee County, not legal, tax, or insurance advice. Disclosure obligations, contract terms, and tax consequences should be reviewed by an attorney or a tax professional. A valuation prepared by a real estate agent is not an appraisal.

Relocating

The house is the easy decision.

Working out what it costs to carry, where the water goes in a storm, and which side of the bridge suits how you actually live takes longer.

Before You Fly Down

Almost everyone I work with buys here before they have spent a summer here.

That is not a criticism. It is the reason these four questions matter more than any listing does.

What does it actually cost to live here

Florida has no state income tax, which is why a lot of people start looking. Then they see an insurance quote. Both of those things are true at the same time, and the net depends entirely on your situation and the specific property. My role is to put real numbers in front of you rather than a sales pitch in either direction.

Where should I actually be looking

Anna Maria and Parrish are forty minutes apart and have nothing in common. One is a barrier island, and the other is new construction on what was farmland. Most people arrive with a picture of the beach in their head and a budget that points somewhere else entirely, and my role is to reconcile the two honestly.

How worried should I be about storms

It is a real question and it deserves a real answer rather than reassurance. Flood zone, elevation, roof age, and whether the windows are impact rated matter more than the county you are in. Two houses on the same street can carry very different risk and very different premiums.

What order do I do all this in

The order matters: lender first, insurance agent second, house third. Doing it the other way around is how people end up under contract on something they cannot insure at a price that works. The guide walks through the sequence step by step.

Questions

What people moving here ask.

How much more does it cost to own a home in Florida than where I live now?

The mortgage is usually the part that looks familiar. Insurance is the part that does not. A homeowners policy on the Gulf Coast commonly runs several times what the same house would cost to insure in the Midwest or the Northeast, and wind coverage is often priced separately. Add flood coverage where it applies, county property taxes, and in many Manatee County communities both HOA dues and a CDD assessment. Florida has no state income tax, which offsets some of this for some people. The only way to know your number is to price a specific address, which I do before you get attached to a house.

Should I rent here first before buying?

For a lot of people moving from out of state, yes, and I will tell you that even though it delays a sale for me. Living here for a season teaches you things a weekend visit cannot, like what the summer is actually like, how long the drive to the beach really takes in February, and whether you want to be on the island or ten minutes from it. If you already know the area well, that logic does not apply and there is no reason to wait.

When is the best time to look?

Inventory and competition both peak in the winter season, roughly January through March, when the seasonal population is here. Summer is quieter, with less to choose from but less competition for it. Neither is clearly better. What matters more is being ready when the right property appears, which means having your financing and your insurance conversation done in advance rather than starting them after you find something.

Do I need to be in Florida to buy a home here?

No. Out of state purchases are routine here and much of the process is handled remotely. I tour properties on video, contracts are signed electronically, and Florida closings are commonly handled by a title company rather than requiring everyone in one room. Most buyers still want to see the house in person before closing, and I would encourage that, but the paperwork is not the obstacle people expect.

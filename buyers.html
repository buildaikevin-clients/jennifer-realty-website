For Buyers

Start here. The home buying process, without the surprises.

Finding the house is the enjoyable part. Understanding what it costs to own, where the water goes in a storm, and what the association has not yet funded is the work that deserves your attention first.

Decided You Would Like To Buy A Home?

So where do you start?

Buying here is the largest purchase most people ever make, and the part that catches them out is rarely the house. It is the flood zone, the insurance quote, the roof with four years left, and the association that has not funded its reserves. My role is to work through all of it with you, so you can move forward with clarity.

01.

Prepare

Before we look at a single house, you talk to a lender and to an insurance agent. In Florida that second call is not a formality at the end. It is a large part of your monthly payment, and I believe you should know your real number before you fall in love with anything. I will introduce you to professionals I trust to be responsive, with no obligation and no pressure to move faster than you want to.

02.

Shop

This is the part most people enjoy, and it should be. We work out what actually matters to you: full time or seasonal, walking distance to the sand or a larger lot, near the water or east where the newer construction is. Then I tell you what I see in every house, including roof age, elevation against the street, whether the windows are impact rated, and what the association holds in reserve. If a home has a problem I will say so, even when it is the one you already love.

Home Search

03.

Offer

Once you have found the right home, my job is to make sure the transaction holds together. I write the offer against what has actually closed nearby rather than against asking prices, then manage the inspection, the appraisal, the insurance binder, and the title work. Transactions here fall apart on one of those four far more often than on price, so I stay on all of them, and you hear from me before you have to ask.

Questions

The five things buyers ask first.

What does a home here cost beyond the mortgage payment?

More than most people expect, and insurance is the reason. A Florida homeowners policy on the Gulf Coast commonly runs several times what the same house would cost to insure inland, and wind coverage is priced separately in many cases. On top of that you may have flood insurance, county property taxes, and in a lot of Manatee County communities both an HOA due and a CDD assessment that shows up on the tax bill. Before you make an offer I get you real numbers for that specific address rather than a rule of thumb.

How do flood zones work and will I need flood insurance?

FEMA maps every property into a flood zone, and the zone plus the elevation of the structure drives both whether a lender requires flood coverage and what it costs. Zone X is the lower risk designation and coverage there is often modest. Zones AE and VE, which cover much of the coastal and low lying parts of the county, are a different conversation, and an elevation certificate can change the premium substantially. I pull the zone for any address you are serious about, and I will tell you when a property needs a surveyor or an insurance agent to look before you commit.

Is it better to buy on the islands or on the mainland?

They behave like two different markets. Island property on Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, Bradenton Beach and Longboat Key carries higher prices, higher insurance, rental income potential, and rules about short term rentals that vary street by street. Mainland Bradenton, Palma Sola and the areas east toward Lakewood Ranch cost less to buy and less to carry, and you trade walking to the sand for a drive. Neither is the right answer on its own. It depends on whether you are living here full time, part time, or renting it out.

What should I know before buying a condo in this area?

Florida changed the rules for condominium associations after 2021, and they matter to buyers. Buildings three stories and taller now face milestone structural inspections and are required to fund reserves based on a structural integrity reserve study rather than voting to waive them. The practical effect is that some associations have raised dues sharply or passed special assessments. Before you go under contract I want to see the association budget, the reserve study, the most recent milestone inspection, and the minutes. A low price on a unit sometimes means a large assessment is coming.

How long does buying a home here usually take?

From accepted offer to closing, a financed purchase in Manatee County generally runs about thirty to forty five days, and cash can close faster. The parts that move that timeline are the inspection period, the appraisal, and lately the insurance binder, because getting a policy quoted and bound has become one of the slower steps rather than a formality. Finding the right house is the part with no schedule. Some people write an offer the first weekend and some look for a year.

General information about the buying process in Manatee County, not legal, tax, or insurance advice. Association documents, flood determinations, insurance quotes, and contract terms should be reviewed by the appropriate licensed professional before you rely on them.

Ten logo directions

Every mark below is built from the palette and the two serifs the website already runs on, so whichever one gets picked drops straight into the site with nothing else to change. Each card shows the full lockup on the page ground, the same lockup reversed on the dark panel color, and the icon alone at 48, 28 and 16 pixels, because a mark that dies at favicon size is not finished.

Tell me the number you like. I will then draw it properly as outlined vector paths, export the favicon, the social preview and a transparent PNG set, and swap it into the site.

Stacked wordmark 01

Her two names happen to be eight letters each, so they stack into a perfect block. The quietest, most brokerage neutral option and the one that ages the slowest.

Strength: timeless · Risk: reads generic without the mark
Ring monogram 02

A thin ring holding a serif JB, wordmark set beside it. The most versatile shape she can own: it survives a stamp, a lapel pin, a yard sign and a 16 pixel browser tab without redrawing.

Strength: works at every size · Risk: circles are common
Gulf horizon 03

Sun sitting on the water inside a soft square. This is the only concept that says Gulf coast without a palm tree, and the accent color finally earns its name.

Strength: sense of place · Risk: leans resort
Arch and key 04

The rounded arch is straight out of Florida Mediterranean architecture, and the serif J inside doubles as the shaft of a key. Property first, coast second.

Strength: says real estate instantly · Risk: J crowds at 16px
Signature and tide 05

Her name in the italic serif with a single tide line drawn beneath it. The warmest option, and the one that feels like a person rather than a firm. Best for a solo agent who sells on relationship.

Strength: personal · Risk: italic loses legibility small
Diamond monogram 06

A rotated square with the initials centered. Sharp, confident, and the single easiest mark on this page to embroider, engrave or emboss.

Strength: physical goods · Risk: reads a little formal
Heron 07

One continuous line for the bird that stands in every Manatee County backwater. The most distinctive mark here and the only one nobody else in her market is likely to be using.

Strength: memorable and ownable · Risk: muddies at 16px
Sail 08

Two triangles that read as a sail on the water and, looked at again, as a rooftop. Geometric, modern, and the cheapest of all of these to reproduce anywhere.

Strength: reads at any size · Risk: least personal
Split initials 09

The initials set large with a hairline between them and the full name underneath. Editorial rather than corporate. This is the direction fashion houses use and it photographs beautifully on a sign.

Strength: editorial and upscale · Risk: wide lockup
Sunrise crest 10

Three arcs rising out of a baseline, framed like a crest. Abstract enough to be a sunrise, a roofline or a shell, which is exactly the ambiguity a good mark wants.

Strength: abstract and calm · Risk: needs the wordmark

Before this becomes real

  • Every mark uses only the deep, gulf, gulf dark, sand and bone tokens already declared at the top of css/styles.css. No new color enters the brand.
  • Type is live text here, not outlines, so the fonts have to load. In the final files the letterforms get converted to vector paths, which is what makes the logo render identically on a sign, a shirt and a fax.
  • The website nav currently uses text only, with assets/favicon.svg as a placeholder J. The chosen mark replaces that file and slots into .nav__brand.
  • Nothing here uses the Preferred Shore logo. Brokerage marks have their own usage rules and hers has to be requested from the firm.
  • A personal mark for a licensed agent still has to sit next to the brokerage name at every point of contact under Florida rule 61J2 10.025. The logo does not satisfy that requirement on its own.
